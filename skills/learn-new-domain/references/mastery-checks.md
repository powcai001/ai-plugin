# Mastery Checks

Use mastery checks throughout the project to build and repair understanding. Passing one surface only proves familiarity with that surface.

## Contents

- Relationship reconstruction
- Explanation and prediction
- Attack and deletion experiments
- Recovery and transfer
- Minimum-time verification route
- Evidence-based levels
- Final oral questions

## 0. Relationship reconstruction

Without notes, require the learner to rebuild:

```text
5-10 essential nouns
-> key verbs attached to each noun
-> relationships expressed as actions
-> observable results
-> failure modes and verifiers
```

Then compare the reconstructed map with actual behavior. Do not penalize different wording; locate missing, false, or disconnected relationships and use the next practice action to repair them.

## 1. Explanation check

Without notes, ask the learner to explain:

- why each noun is essential and what action makes it operational;
- the minimum noun-action system and its core loop;
- the difference between framework and strategy;
- why the evaluation criteria were chosen before the route;
- where best practices stop applying;
- what independently verifies the result.

Pass when the explanation includes relationships, trade-offs, and boundaries rather than definitions alone.

## 2. Prediction check

Before running an action, ask for:

```text
Expected result:
Most likely failure:
Evidence that would change my mind:
```

Pass when predictions are specific enough to be contradicted by the result.

## 3. Attack check

Require at least one counterexample or adversarial input for each important claim or feature. Ask the learner to distinguish:

- expected rejection;
- unexpected failure;
- a result that falsifies the current model.

## 4. Deletion experiment

Remove or disable one component, rule, test, source, or step. Ask:

- What becomes impossible to know or do?
- Which failure now becomes invisible?
- Can the learner reconstruct the missing component from first principles?

Use safe, reversible deletion and preserve a recovery path.

## 5. Recovery check

Introduce a deliberate failure or corrupted state. Require the learner to:

1. detect it from evidence;
2. identify the failed assumption;
3. restore a known-good state;
4. verify recovery independently;
5. add a prevention or detection mechanism.

## 6. Transfer check

Give a new problem with different surface details. The learner must rebuild:

- goal and boundary;
- framework;
- evaluation criteria;
- strategy;
- relevant best practices and tools;
- failure modes;
- verifier.

Pass only when the learner adapts the method rather than copying the previous artifact.

## Minimum-time verification route

Use the cheapest check that can expose the next misunderstanding:

```text
Reconstruct -> Predict and execute -> Explain evidence
-> Attack -> Delete and recover -> Transfer
```

- Stop repeating checks that no longer reveal a gap.
- When a check fails, repair the specific relationship and rerun only the smallest relevant action.
- Do not skip deletion, recovery, or transfer merely to shorten elapsed time; those checks distinguish familiarity from operational mastery.

## Evidence-based levels

### Orientation

Can recognize terms and follow an example. Cannot yet choose or verify independently.

### Assisted practice

Can complete the loop with prompts and explain major decisions afterward.

### Independent application

Can define criteria, select a route, attack assumptions, and verify results without being given the structure step by step.

### Transfer

Can apply the loop to a different problem, explain what changed, and design a valid verifier.

Treat **Transfer** as the default mastery threshold. Report the evidence observed, the current level, and one next gap. Do not use percentages or motivational scores.

## Final oral questions

1. Which 5-10 nouns form the smallest complete system, and why is each necessary?
2. Which verbs connect them, and what state or output does each action change?
3. Which relationship did the practice project make clearer?
4. Which meta-word expanded your search space the most, and which one reduced it?
5. What decision would have changed under a different evaluation criterion?
6. Which best practice did you reject, and why?
7. What failure did the AI fail to predict?
8. What evidence was independent of the proposed answer?
9. What did the deletion and recovery experiments reveal?
10. What transferred unchanged, and what had to be redesigned?
